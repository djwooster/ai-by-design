If you've looked into building an app with AI, you've probably felt overwhelmed: a hundred different tools, a thousand YouTube videos, everyone doing it differently, jargon everywhere. A lot of it is probably genuinely useful and helpful, but you only need a fraction of it all to actually build great apps. Here's the short version of what I'd tell a friend.

1. Your three tools (5 min)

- Claude: your thinking partner. You'll use it to shape your idea, plan your app and write your prompts. This is the part most people skip, and it's the part that makes everything else work.
- Claude Code: the builder. It writes and edits the code on your computer, and explains what it did when you ask. We'll set it up on Day 4.
- Expo Go: a free iPhone app that opens the app you're building, live, while you work on it. Change something on your computer and you see it on your phone seconds later.

Why these and not the others? No-code app builders get you a demo fast, but you hit a wall the moment you want something to look or work exactly the way you imagined. Claude Code builds a real app you own, file by file. And Expo means you can start on a Mac or a Windows PC and see your app on your iPhone today. You only need a Mac and Apple's $99/year developer account later, when you're ready to publish.

Today: get a Claude plan that includes Claude Code, and install Expo Go on your phone. Ignore every other tool for now.

2. Collect what you like (15 min)

Clarity is the whole game. Before you write a single prompt, you need to know what you want, and you get there by looking at things you like.

When I started FocusMode, I knew it was a timer, so the function was simple. The look took more thought. I went with an old-school style inspired by Teenage Engineering, like a physical device on your desk. I found it by digging through Pinterest, saving screenshots, and later handing them to Claude Code.

Your turn: save 5–10 screenshots into one folder. Aim for three kinds:

- The vibe: one or two images that capture the overall feeling. They don't have to be apps. A product, a poster, a car dashboard.
- Screens: apps that do something similar to yours, or that you just love using.
- Details: a button, a font, a chart, a transition you keep noticing.

For each one, write one line about what you like. "Chunky buttons that feel physical." "Lots of empty space." Those lines are worth more than the screenshots, because they turn your taste into words Claude can use.

3. Write your vision with Claude (10 min)

Open Claude, paste this, and fill in the brackets:

"I want to build an iPhone app. My idea: [your idea]. It's for [who]. Ask me questions one at a time until you can write a one-paragraph vision for the app, plus a short list of what the first version should and shouldn't include. Keep the first version small."

Attach your screenshots along with your one-line notes. Answer its questions honestly, including "I don't know yet." That's what the questions are for.

A good vision is specific enough that someone else could picture the app. Something like: "A focus timer that feels like a small device on your desk. One big button to start a session, the time left always visible, nothing else competing for your attention."

One rule for the first version: if you can't describe the main thing it does in one sentence, keep cutting. You can add everything else later.

Save the paragraph, and keep this Claude chat open. We'll pick it up again tomorrow.
